# 14 · Simulation

What the prototype runs against, what that can establish, and — the part that
matters — **what it structurally cannot**.

---

## 14.1 · Why simulation at all

Three reasons, in order of force:

1. **You cannot inject a sensor fault into a real vehicle at speed.** The entire
   fault suite requires a plant you can lie to on purpose.
2. **Ground truth.** Every claim of the form *"the estimate was 0.02 m and the
   truth was 4.199 m"* requires knowing the truth. A real vehicle does not
   supply it to four decimals.
3. **Reproducibility.** The evidence pack's claims must be re-derivable from one
   command. Real driving is not re-runnable.

---

## 14.2 · What it runs against today — `SyntheticDrivingEnv`

**[FACT** — `training/environment.py`.**]**

> **Lane-following on the pipeline's own kinematic model.**

That sentence is the whole limitation, stated by the code itself. The plant is a
**kinematic bicycle model** — the same family of model as the twin, the corpus,
and the process model inside the filter.

### Two design decisions in it worth knowing

**The observation is the feature vector, not the raw state**, produced by *the
same call* the deployed policy makes:

> Sharing the function rather than the formula is what makes train/inference
> **feature drift impossible rather than merely unlikely**.

**The Trust Index is fixed at 1.0 during training**, with a stated reason:

> The trust signal is produced by L3 from a calibration corpus that does not
> exist inside this environment, and feeding the policy a fabricated confidence
> score would teach it to condition on a number that means something different at
> runtime. **A constant is the one value that teaches it nothing.**

**[INTERPRETATION]** Both are examples of the project's most characteristic move:
when a signal is unavailable, supply the value that carries *no* information
rather than a plausible-looking substitute. The same instinct refused
`RAIN_NIGHT` and refused a default for every safety threshold.

## 14.3 · The distinction that governs every result

**[FACT** — `CREDIBILITY_MATRIX.md`, quoted in full because it is the most
important paragraph in the project:**]**

> The digital twin, the calibration corpus, and the trained policy **all descend
> from the same kinematic bicycle model**. The generator and the judge agree by
> construction. Every `[M-syn]` row therefore demonstrates **that machinery
> runs — not that it is correct**.

Concretely, the same model appears **four** times:

```
                    kinematic bicycle model
                             │
        ┌────────────┬───────┴────────┬──────────────┐
        ▼            ▼                ▼              ▼
   the PLANT    L2's process     the TWIN      the CORPUS
   being driven     model      (trained on it) (generated through it)
```

**So when the twin predicts the plant accurately, that is not evidence the twin
is good.** It is evidence that two implementations of the same equations agree.

**[INTERPRETATION]** This is the single strongest criticism available against the
prototype, and the project makes it against itself, at the top of its own
credibility document. Any reader forming a view should weigh that both ways:
the limitation is severe, *and* a project that leads with it is behaving well.

## 14.4 · What simulation **can** establish

| Can establish | Because |
|---|---|
| **The machinery runs** | The pipeline completes 100,000 ticks and produces evidence |
| **Structural properties** | `[M-code]` — true regardless of plant |
| **A mechanism responds to a fault it was built for** | Dropout → HALT at +40 vs departure at +73 |
| **A mechanism does *not* respond** | Five detectors silent on drift — a **refutation** is valid on synthetic data |
| **Relative comparisons under identical conditions** | Single channel 0.8387 m vs redundant 0.0168 m, same seed, same fault |
| **Implementation errors** | The corpus generator's own words: *"It can expose an implementation error"* |

**The fourth row is the underrated one.** A *negative* result transfers far
better than a positive one: if a detector cannot see a fault on data generated by
the very model it assumes, it will not do better on harder data.

---

## 14.5 · What simulation **cannot** establish

| Cannot establish | Why |
|---|---|
| **That the twin is accurate** | It was trained on the plant it is predicting |
| **That the corpus describes reality** | Generated through that twin |
| **Coverage of the conformal guarantee** | Exchangeability against a *real* distribution is untested — and is already violated in-house |
| **False-positive / false-negative rates** | Would be rates against a plant we wrote |
| **That the process model is adequate** | The plant *is* the process model |
| **Timing** | A-2's 10 ms is an assumption; simulation runs on different hardware, off the tick |
| **Sensor realism** | Real sensors have correlated, structured, non-Gaussian failure modes |

The corpus generator prints its own caveat on every run:

> Synthetic corpus from a twin trained on synthetic kinematics. **It can expose an
> implementation error; it cannot establish that coverage holds on real driving.**

## 14.6 · The gap between simulation and reality

| Dimension | `SyntheticDrivingEnv` | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Dynamics** | Kinematic bicycle | Suspension, tyre slip, load transfer, drivetrain lag |
| **Sensor noise** | Independent Gaussian | Correlated, structured, non-Gaussian; multipath, occlusion, dropout bursts |
| **Sensor failure** | Injected on purpose, one channel | Correlated across channels — shared bus, supply, weather |
| **Environment** | A lane | Other road users, geometry, weather, light |
| **Timing** | Deterministic, injected clock | Jitter, drops, scheduling |
| **Ground truth** | Exact | Unavailable |

**The row that most affects the safety argument is sensor failure correlation.**
The redundancy argument assumes independence. A shared supplier, a shared bus, a
shared firmware image, or simply *the same fog* breaks it — and that is precisely
threat `T1'`, where two coordinated liars make the monitor blame the honest
channel.

## 14.7 · CARLA — what changes, and what does not

**[FACT** — `docs/CARLA_PLAN.md`.**]**

CARLA is the only item on the backlog that can move a row from `[M-syn]` to
`[M-ext]`, because it is a plant this project did not write.

**Six predictions were written down before running** — falsifiable, dated, and
kept whichever way they go:

| | Prediction |
|---|---|
| P1 | **OD-8 gets worse.** L6's scores land outside the CARLA corpus |
| P2 | **The twin is badly wrong.** It learned bicycle kinematics; CARLA has suspension and slip |
| P3 | **L7a finally fires.** The corridor bound is reachable in real driving |
| P4 | **The gate census inverts.** L6 stops being silent and starts vetoing constantly |
| P5 | **Wall 3 does not bite.** The bicycle process model is adequate *for a car* |
| P6 | **The fail-safe halts more often.** Real sensors drop frames |

**[INTERPRETATION]** P3 and P4 are the ones that matter. Two of three gates
currently never object; CARLA is where the three-gate independence claim gets its
first real test — or where the paper's contribution 2 needs rewriting further
than it already does.

### What CARLA still will not establish

- **Real sensors.** CARLA's sensor models are models.
- **Real dynamics.** Better than a bicycle model; still a simulation.
- **A second *domain*.** CARLA is automotive, so OD-11's wall 4 stays open — the
  rename needs a warehouse, not a better car.
- **Certification.** `[M-ext]` against one simulator, one town, one seed.

The plan says so itself: *"one town is one town … `[M-ext]` earned here is
narrower than it sounds and the matrix should say so in the row, not in a
footnote."*

## 14.8 · The simulator-specific discipline

**[FACT** — `CARLA_PLAN.md` §3.**]** The data-split protocol was written for
comma2k19 — a *fixed* dataset, where the risk is accidental overlap.

**A generator inverts the risk.** You cannot accidentally reuse a segment; you
can **regenerate TEST after seeing the result**, and nothing in the data or the
code catches it.

So the plan adds one rule above everything else: **commit the route manifest and
the seed to git before the first frame is rendered**, and make TEST-once
mechanical — an append-only log of TEST manifest digests, and a second run
against the same digest refuses.

> Not tamper-proof — anyone can delete the log — and that is not the point. The
> point is that re-running TEST becomes **a deliberate act someone performs on
> purpose**, rather than something that happens because a number looked
> disappointing on a Friday.

## 14.9 · You should know this before moving on

**The four-way coincidence:** the plant, L2's process model, the twin, and the
corpus are **all the same bicycle model**. Every `[M-syn]` result must be read
through that.

**Negative results transfer; positive results do not.** *"Five detectors are
silent"* is strong evidence. *"The twin predicts well"* is nearly none.

**Questions you should be able to answer**

1. Why does the twin predicting the plant accurately tell you almost nothing?
2. Why is a *refutation* on synthetic data more transferable than a confirmation?
3. Why is the Trust Index pinned to 1.0 during training rather than estimated?
4. Which simulation-vs-reality gap most threatens the redundancy argument?
5. Why does a *generator* need a stricter data-split discipline than a fixed
   dataset?

**Misconception to avoid**

> *"It works in simulation, so it mostly works."*
>
> The simulation is the pipeline's own model of the world. When it agrees with
> the pipeline, that is **two implementations of the same equations agreeing** —
> not evidence about driving. The project states this at the top of its own
> credibility document, and the only thing that changes it is a plant nobody here
> wrote.
