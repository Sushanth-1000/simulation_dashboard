# E17 — Operating-Regime Analysis

**This document reports a negative result.** The regime hypothesis that motivated the whole
instrumentation effort is **not supported**, and the pooled statistic that appears to support it is a
Simpson's paradox.

---

## 1 · Why regime was measured at all

`E17_SECOND_POLICY_DECISION.md` returned **CONDITIONAL_GO** with one condition:

> the 30-seed sweep must record **operating regime** — veto rate and mean speed per run — as a
> covariate, so the speed-channel boundary can be characterised rather than averaged away.

The motivating observation: at n = 1, the two speed faults classified identically on P1 and P3 and
diverged on P2 — and P2 was vetoed on ~70 % of ticks and drove at half speed. The proposed
mechanism: **a speed bias on a vehicle whose speed is already being clamped by governance has a
masked signature.**

**H-regime:** speed-channel discriminability at L1 decreases as veto rate rises.

Pre-registered as **secondary and exploratory**, with this caveat written before the sweep:

> It was generated by looking at P2 at n = 1, so it cannot be confirmed by the same observation. A
> positive result is a hypothesis worth a designed experiment, not a finding.

That caveat turned out to be the important sentence.

## 2 · Regime as realised

| policy | veto rate median [min, max] | mean speed median [min, max] | regime counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 0.0250 [0.0025, 0.5100] | 10.65 [5.62, 13.65] | DEGRADED 2, INTERMEDIATE 36, NORMAL 142 |
| P2 | 0.8662 [0.6050, 0.9900] | 3.32 [2.22, 11.61] | **DEGRADED 180** |
| P3 | 0.0075 [0.0000, 0.4925] | 11.07 [5.69, 14.06] | INTERMEDIATE 30, NORMAL 150 |

Cut points (`< 0.10` NORMAL, `≥ 0.50` DEGRADED) were fixed in the pre-registration and are **not**
revised here. They landed where intended — in the gap between the policies.

**P2 occupies [0.605, 0.990]. P1 and P3 occupy [0.000, 0.510].** The ranges **do not overlap.**

## 3 · The pooled result — apparently confirmatory

Spearman, `D_L1` against faulted-arm veto rate, all 90 runs, permutation *p*:

| fault | ρ vs veto rate | *p* | ρ vs mean speed | *p* |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| speed_bias | **−0.341** | 0.0008 | +0.335 | 0.0013 |
| speed_stuck | **−0.457** | 0.0001 | +0.399 | < 0.0001 |

Both negative against veto rate, both positive against mean speed, all four significant. **Exactly
the predicted direction and comfortably significant.** Taken at face value this confirms H-regime.

**It should not be taken at face value.**

## 4 · The confound check — and the reversal

Because the three policies barely share a veto range, a pooled correlation cannot distinguish
*regime* from *policy*. Within each policy, veto rate still varies. So the same correlation was
computed within policy.

| fault | P1 | P2 | P3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **speed_bias** | undefined (`D_L1` = 1.000 on all 30) | **ρ = +0.836, *p* < 0.0001** | undefined (`D_L1` = 1.000 on all 30) |
| **speed_stuck** | ρ = −0.043, *p* = 0.82 | ρ = −0.338, *p* = 0.069 | ρ = −0.206, *p* = 0.27 |

**Within the only policy where `speed_bias` has any variance to correlate, the sign flips: +0.836,
strongly positive, against a pooled −0.341.** For `speed_stuck`, every within-policy correlation is
non-significant, including P2's at α = 0.05.

**This is a textbook Simpson's paradox.** The pooled negative correlation is produced entirely by
P2 sitting at low `D_L1` and high veto rate while P1 and P3 sit at high `D_L1` and low veto rate. It
is a **between-policy** difference wearing a within-run covariate's clothing.

## 5 · Verdict

# H-regime: NOT SUPPORTED

The pooled statistic is an artefact of policy stratification. The within-policy evidence is null for
`speed_stuck` and **opposite in sign** for `speed_bias`.

**Had the pre-registration's pooled Spearman been run and reported without the confound check, this
study would have reported a significant confirmation of a hypothesis its own data contradicts.** The
check is recorded here as a stated robustness analysis, not as the primary test, and it changes the
conclusion.

## 6 · What this does and does not settle

**Settled:** the n = 1 speed-fault divergence between P2 and P1/P3 is real and reproduces at n = 30
— P2's speed faults are genuinely unstable (`speed_stuck` modal `A(f)` = L2b at 57 %, `speed_bias`
modal L2a at 40 %) where P1/P3 are stable. **The divergence is real.**

**Not settled:** *why*. The masking mechanism remains a plausible unfalsified story. This design
cannot test it, because veto rate is almost perfectly confounded with policy identity, and the two
policies with clean speed results have **zero variance in `D_L1`** (1.000 on every seed) — there is
nothing left for a covariate to explain.

**The condition from `E17_SECOND_POLICY_DECISION.md` is discharged.** Regime was measured rather than
averaged away, and the answer is that this experimental design cannot separate regime from policy.
That is a real answer, and it is more useful than the false confirmation the pooled number offered.

## 7 · What would actually test it

Not more seeds — more seeds of a confounded design buy nothing. The test needs veto rate varied
**within a single policy**, by sweeping a gate threshold rather than by swapping checkpoints. That
severs veto rate from policy identity and is the designed experiment the pre-registration said a
positive result would warrant.

**It is out of scope under the current freeze** (it changes thresholds) and is recorded as the next
question, not run.

## 8 · Honest note on multiplicity

Four pooled correlations plus six within-policy correlations were computed. Only the two pooled
`veto_rate` correlations were pre-registered. The within-policy set is a **stated robustness check
that overturned the pre-registered result**, not a search for significance — it was run because the
non-overlapping ranges in §2 made the pooled statistic uninterpretable, and it was run before any
conclusion was written. No correction is applied to it because it is not being used to claim an
effect; it is being used to withdraw one.
